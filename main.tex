\begin{center}
    \bfseries\Large Abstract
\end{center}


Vietnamese vaccine information is published in documents that vary in structure and are often written for specialist readers. VaxiS is a Vietnamese-language retrieval-augmented generation system designed to help users locate relevant passages and trace an answer to its documentary evidence. The repository contains a structure-aware Markdown indexing pipeline, lexical and dense retrieval paths, and a hybrid retrieval pipeline that combines dense and learned sparse rankings before optional reranking. A shared generator uses the retrieved chunks as context and tracks citations to chunk identifiers. The experimental design follows six configurations in order: Naive, Advanced, Hybrid (baseline), Table-aware, Graph RAG, and Agent RAG. The working benchmark contains 300 Vietnamese questions spanning single-hop, multi-hop, and table-reasoning tasks. \pending{For the final three configurations, implementation and evaluation documentation are pending. \textbf{Keywords}: Vaccine information, Vietnamese question answering, retrieval-augmented generation, hybrid retrieval, evidence attribution.

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}
VaxiS addresses a practical information access problem. A user may know a vaccine by its product name and have a specific question, while the relevant source is a long instruction leaflet or regulatory document. Locating the applicable passage requires identifying the product, recognizing the relevant section and interpreting any qualifications recorded in nearby text or table notes. The project supports natural-language lookup of information in the collected vaccine usage instructions. The topic taxonomy in the older testdata module belongs to its dataset-generation workflow and does not define the current system's supported question categories.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) brings evidence from an external collection into the generation process~\cite{lewis2020rag}. In this setting, the evidence should be accessible to the reader so the answer can be checked against the source. Producing a fluent response, citing a retrieved passage, and supporting each factual claim are distinct requirements. Research on generation with citations similarly evaluates attribution as a quality dimension alongside answer correctness~\cite{gao2023citations}.

The primary intended users are ordinary citizens. Healthcare workers and healthcare students are secondary users who may use the system for document lookup. The scope is general access to published information. Diagnosis, individual treatment decisions, and processing personal medical records fall outside the project scope.

\subsection{Objectives}
The main research question is how to retrieve relevant evidence from Vietnamese vaccine documents and use it to generate a comprehensible answer with traceable citations, including when the available evidence is incomplete.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Prepare a searchable corpus from available vaccine source documents, retaining the document identifiers and section metadata needed to trace evidence.
\item Compare lexical, dense, and hybrid retrieval, then assess the proposed Table-aware, Graph RAG, and Agent RAG extensions.
\item Generate Vietnamese responses that cite the supplied evidence and communicate when the evidence is insufficient.
\item Assess retrieval quality, answer quality, citation support, and abstention using documented datasets and reproducible run configurations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Automated Access to Vaccine Information}
Li et al.\ developed VaxBot-HPV around a curated knowledge base and a fine-tuned GPT model for HPV vaccine questions. Their evaluation included answer relevancy and faithfulness, two criteria that inform the assessment of VaxiS~\cite{li2025vaxbothpv}.

The Vaccine Information Resource Assistant (VIRA), developed by Johns Hopkins and IBM Research, uses natural language understanding to match questions to expert-vetted responses about COVID-19 vaccination. Its English and Spanish interfaces illustrate the value of organizing answers around user concerns and making reviewed content accessible in the user's language~\cite{jhuvira}.

K-Bot provides culturally tailored HPV vaccine information in Korean and English. Its authors designed dialogs around evidence-based content adapted to their intended audience. This work motivates attention to phrasing and accessibility in a Vietnamese-language interface~\cite{kim2025kbot}. VaxiS focuses on source-grounded question answering over the Vietnamese documents available to the project, with provenance carried through retrieval and generation.

\subsection{Evidence-Grounded Question Answering over Vaccine Documents}
BM25 uses corpus statistics and document length to score lexical matches~\cite{robertson2009bm25}. Dense retrieval instead ranks passages through similarity in an embedding space. BGE-M3 provides both dense representations and learned sparse lexical weights in a multilingual encoder~\cite{chen2024m3}. Reciprocal rank fusion combines the positions assigned by multiple retrieval streams on a common scale~\cite{cormack2009rrf}.

Evidence can also cross representational boundaries. HybridQA studies questions that require combining tables with associated passages~\cite{chen2020hybridqa}. Graph RAG research illustrates how entities and relationships extracted from documents can organize distributed evidence for a query, ranging from hierarchical community summarization~\cite{edge2024graphrag} to dual-level graph indexing that pairs low-level entity grounding with high-level relational neighborhood traversal~\cite{guo2024lightrag}. ReAct illustrates how a language model can interleave decisions with actions that gather information~\cite{yao2023react}. These works motivate candidate extensions to the VaxiS pipeline. The specific designs and empirical effects of those extensions require project implementation records and completed runs.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Overview of the Vaccine Question-Answering System}
The documented pipeline has a document-preparation and indexing stage followed by question answering. Document preparation collects PDFs from the Drug Administration of Vietnam public service portal and converts them to Markdown using Google's Gemini. Indexing turns cleaned Markdown into identifiable chunks, adds document and section metadata, and persists the chunks in Parquet. The vector indexing path prepares dense and sparse representations in Qdrant. During question answering, the selected retriever ranks chunks. The generator receives the question and selected evidence, produces a Vietnamese response, and tracks the chunk identifiers cited by the response. The evaluation tooling also supports scoring ranked results stored from an earlier run.

\subsection{Vaccine Document Processing Module}
The team collected vaccine usage instructions from the Drug Administration of Vietnam public service portal at (\url{https://dichvucong.dav.gov.vn/congbothuoc/index}) on September 18, 2026. The listing was obtained with an HTTP GET request whose limit parameter was increased to retrieve all 123 vaccine listings in one request. The preparation work was divided into three parts. PDFs were trimmed to retain the information needed for the project, removing label pages and other unnecessary material. Each file was uploaded individually to the Gemini website and converted to Markdown using the website's default model. \subsection{Vaccine Knowledge Graph Module}
\label{subsec:kg_module}
Graph RAG constitutes the fifth configuration in the experimental sequence. Whereas lexical and dense vector retrievers score passages as isolated textual units, clinical immunization inquiries frequently require connecting evidence distributed across non-adjacent sections of a package insert—such as linking a residual excipient listed under qualitative composition to a hypersensitivity warning under contraindications—or comparing pharmacological profiles across distinct vaccine products. To model these cross-passage dependencies explicitly, we construct a domain-specific biomedical knowledge graph following the dual-level graph indexing paradigm of LightRAG~\cite{guo2024lightrag}.

\paragraph{Structure-preserving pre-chunked ingestion.}
Conventional graph construction pipelines re-segment raw documents into uniform token windows, which severs provenance alignment with the passage index and fragments multi-column clinical tables. To preserve strict structural correspondence with the document corpus, the knowledge graph ingestion pipeline operates directly over the pre-partitioned corpus chunks rather than re-splitting raw document text. Prior to entity extraction, consecutive table-bearing sub-chunks belonging to the same document and section heading are merged and deduplicated at boundary rows. Across the 75 vaccine usage leaflets, this consolidation collapses the 490 table-bearing sub-chunks into 238 complete tables (classified into 92 clinical guidance tables and 146 statistical immunogenicity tables), reducing the 1,744 raw corpus chunks to 1,492 consolidated text and table units. Because raw HTML markup and dense numerical matrices degrade named-entity recognition, each consolidated table is transformed into entity-rich Vietnamese Subject--Verb--Object prose prior to graph extraction—preserving exhaustive row-level clinical rules for clinical tables and study-level comparative summaries for statistical trial tables.

\paragraph{Clinical entity ontology and graph extraction.}
Entity and relationship extraction is governed by a domain-specific ontology of twelve clinical immunization categories designed for Vietnamese regulatory leaflets (Table~\ref{tab:kg_ontology}). Given each consolidated passage or verbalized table, an instruction-tuned language model extracts typed entity vertices $v = (e_v, \tau_v, d_v)$ and directed relational edges $r_{uv} = (u, v, \mathcal{K}_{uv}, d_{uv}, w_{uv})$ under strict JSON schema constraints, where $e_v$ denotes the canonical Vietnamese surface form, $\tau_v$ is the assigned clinical ontology class, $d_v$ and $d_{uv}$ are synthesized Vietnamese clinical descriptions, $\mathcal{K}_{uv}$ is a set of relational keywords, and $w_{uv} \in \mathbb{R}^+$ denotes edge weight. During incremental graph construction, entity mentions sharing a normalized canonical name across different passages are resolved into a single vertex by concatenating their clinical descriptions and accumulating their documentary provenance pointers. Across all 75 vaccine leaflets (1,492 consolidated chunks), the resulting knowledge graph comprises 3,572 unique entity vertices and 1,830 directed relational edges. Of these, 831 entity vertices (23.26\%) and 81 relational edges (4.43\%) aggregate evidence from two or more distinct source chunks, establishing multi-passage and cross-vaccine connectivity hubs around core products and shared clinical concepts (for example, \emph{Prevenar~13} with degree 91, \emph{Qdenga} with degree 45, and \emph{Nimenrix} and \emph{Varilrix} with degree 43 each).

\paragraph{Dual-database storage and unified embedding space.}
The extracted knowledge graph is maintained in a synchronized dual-storage architecture that pairs a relational property-graph engine with a multi-granular dense vector store:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Topological property graph (Neo4j):} Stores the 3,572 entity vertices (indexed by B-tree and full-text search over canonical entity identifiers) and the 1,830 directed edges carrying relational keywords, synthesized descriptions, edge weights, and source chunk pointers.
\item \textbf{Multi-granular semantic vector index (Qdrant):} Indexes dense vector representations across three parallel collections covering the 1,492 consolidated text chunks, the 3,572 entity descriptions, and the 1,830 relational triplets, using cosine similarity with a minimum retrieval threshold of $0.2$.
\end{enumerate}
To prevent representation drift between passage retrieval and graph retrieval while conserving accelerator memory, all three graph vector collections are encoded into the exact same $1,024$-dimensional multilingual embedding space (BGE-M3, maximum sequence length $8,192$) used by the primary hybrid passage index. During inference, a thread-safe shared encoder architecture allows both the passage retriever and the knowledge-graph retriever to execute against a single in-memory embedding model instance.

\paragraph{Deterministic provenance alignment and verified subgraph example.}
Because the graph indexing engine assigns internal content hashes to ingested text segments while tracking only each segment's parent document identifier and sequential position, direct citation requires a reverse provenance transformation. By replaying the deterministic document-level sorting and table-consolidation order over the canonical corpus store, every internal graph segment pointer $(\text{document}, \text{order index})$ is mapped bijectively back to its canonical corpus chunk identifier—as well as the list of constituent sub-chunk identifiers for merged tables. Across the entire corpus, this reverse mapping achieves $100\%$ resolution ($1,492/1,492$ segments). \subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation Module}
Naive uses the repository's BM25 retrieval path over the chunk corpus. The evaluation CLI selects it with \artifact{--baseline}. Hybrid is the study baseline against which subsequent extensions are compared. Advanced embeds the original query with BGE-M3 and searches the dense Qdrant index before passing the top-ranked chunks to the shared generator.

Hybrid queries the BGE-M3 dense and learned sparse indexes. The implementation aggregates candidate ranks using weighted reciprocal rank fusion,
\[
 S(d)=\sum_{i:\,d\in L_i}\frac{w_i}{c+\mathrm{rank}_i(d)},
\]
where \(L_i\) is a candidate ranking, \(w_i\) its weight and \(c\) a smoothing constant. Hybrid uses the \artifact{BAAI/bge-m3} embedding checkpoint and the \artifact{BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3} cross-encoder reranker. The current configuration defaults to 50 candidates from each stream, a fusion constant of 60, 30 fused candidates, a final depth of five and enabled reranking. These are code defaults, and the experiment manifest must establish the settings actually used. The run record must identify the candidate depths, fusion settings, reranker checkpoint and final evidence depth.

Table-aware is the fourth configuration and is intended to measure retrieval and grounding on questions that depend on tabular structure. It keeps the Hybrid retrieval pipeline (BGE-M3 dense and sparse indexes, weighted reciprocal rank fusion and cross-encoder reranking) and changes how tabular evidence is indexed and surfaced. The shared chunker may split one table across several chunks, which separates rows from their column headers and footnotes. Table-aware therefore adds a preprocessing step before embedding and a merge step after fusion.

\paragraph{Index-time table preprocessing.}
Chunks flagged as tables are grouped when consecutive chunks of the same document share the same section header. Their HTML or Markdown fragments are merged into one table, and rows duplicated at chunk boundaries by the chunk overlap are removed. The identifiers of the merged chunks are stored in the chunk metadata so that evidence remains traceable to the original chunks.

Each merged table is then classified as \artifact{CLINICAL} or \artifact{STATISTICAL}. Clinical tables cover composition, dosage, schedule, adverse reactions, contraindications and storage. Statistical tables cover immunogenicity and efficacy results. The classifier checks keywords in the section header and table head, then the share of numeric cells (statistical at 0.45 or above), then the document category, and finally a 0.35 numeric-ratio threshold.

The language model \artifact{gpt-oss-120b} rewrites each table as Vietnamese prose, using a prompt chosen by table kind. Clinical tables are rewritten row by row, preserving every row, column, dose, frequency and footnote and naming the vaccine in each sentence. Statistical tables are summarized as one overview paragraph that names the study and control vaccines, age groups, pathogens or serotypes, and measures such as GMT, GMC and protective efficacy, without listing every number. Summaries are cached by a SHA-256 key over the document, header, table kind, model and merged table text. On a cache miss without LLM fallback, the merged table text is used alone.

Table-aware uses the \artifact{SUMMARIZE_DUAL} strategy by default. The prose summary and the original merged HTML table are stored together in a single chunk. The dense representation of this chunk benefits from the semantic summary, which improves matching with natural-language questions, while the sparse lexical representation can match exact terms and values from the original table. The generator receives the same chunk, so exact cell values remain available for answering.

\paragraph{Query-time table merging.}
The Table-aware configuration enables table chunk merging at query time. Retrieved table chunks that share a document and section header are merged after fusion and before reranking, and overlapping rows are removed. The merged chunk records its source chunk identifiers, so citations can still be mapped to the original chunks.

\paragraph{Graph-augmented retrieval and hybrid rank fusion.}
In the Graph RAG configuration, retrieval combines dual-level knowledge-graph traversal with the table-aware hybrid passage retriever rather than relying on the graph engine to generate unverified free-text answers. Given a Vietnamese clinical question $q$, an instruction-tuned language model (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) decomposes the inquiry into low-level entity keywords (specific vaccine names, antigens, or age groups) and high-level relational themes (cross-vaccine comparisons, contraindication mechanisms, or concomitant schedules). These keywords are embedded with the shared BGE-M3 encoder to drive a dual-level retrieval process over the knowledge graph:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Low-level entity-centric retrieval} queries the entity vector collection for the top $K_g = 20$ matching vertices and traverses their one-hop incident edges in the Neo4j property graph to gather local neighborhood attributes.
\item \emph{High-level relationship-centric retrieval} queries the relationship vector collection for the top $K_g = 20$ relational triplets and retrieves their connected endpoint vertices to capture broader thematic associations.
\item \emph{Provenance passage selection and token budgeting} prunes the retrieved entity descriptions and relational statements under independent context budgets ($1,500$ tokens for entities and $1,500$ tokens for relationships) and selects up to $K_c = 5$ supporting documentary chunks traced from the provenance pointers of the activated subgraph elements.
\end{enumerate}

The graph retrieval branch emits two distinct evidence structures: (a) a ranked list of up to $K_c = 5$ provenance-resolved documentary chunks $L_{\text{KG}}$, where each segment is mapped back to its canonical corpus chunk identifier and assigned a reciprocal rank score; and (b) a structured Vietnamese knowledge-graph context block summarizing the matched entities (\emph{\vn{Thực thể liên quan}}) and directed relational triplets (\emph{\vn{Mối quan hệ}}).

To integrate these graph outputs with the table-aware hybrid retriever, the system supports two fusion strategies:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Context-Append Fusion:} Retains the top-$k$ ($k=5$) passages selected by the table-aware hybrid retriever and cross-encoder reranker unchanged, and appends the structured knowledge-graph context block as an auxiliary $(k+1)$-th reference block for the generator.
\item \textbf{Alias-Aware Reciprocal Rank Fusion and Reranking:} Combines the top $K_{\text{rrf}} = 30$ pre-reranked candidate passages $L_{\text{hyb}}$ from the table-aware hybrid stream with the $K_c = 5$ provenance-resolved passages $L_{\text{KG}}$ from the knowledge-graph stream prior to cross-encoder reranking. Because a passage surfaced through graph provenance may correspond to an individual sub-chunk within a consolidated multi-chunk table in $L_{\text{hyb}}$, the fusion operator maps every constituent sub-chunk identifier to its canonical merged table identifier $d^* \in \mathrm{Aliases}(d^*)$ and computes an alias-aware cross-branch Reciprocal Rank Fusion score ($c = 60$):
\[
S_{\text{GraphRRF}}(d^*) = \frac{\mathbb{I}[d^* \in L_{\text{hyb}}]}{c + \mathrm{rank}_{\text{hyb}}(d^*)} + \frac{\mathbb{I}[\exists\, d \in \mathrm{Aliases}(d^*): d \in L_{\text{KG}}]}{c + \mathrm{rank}_{\text{KG}}(d)}.
\]
Whenever a graph-traced passage matches a candidate in $L_{\text{hyb}}$, the dual table-aware payload (prose summary paired with the complete HTML table) is preserved and receives the combined RRF boost. The unified candidate pool is then re-ordered by the cross-encoder reranker (BGE-Reranker-v2-M3) to select the final top-$k$ ($k=5$) documentary chunks, after which the structured knowledge-graph summary block is appended at position $k+1$. If no graph entities or relationships exceed the similarity threshold, the structured graph block is omitted and the pipeline falls back gracefully to the top-$k$ reranked documentary chunks.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Source Corpus and Structural Distribution}
\label{subsec:corpus_distribution}
The documentary foundation of VaxiS is a curated corpus of 75 official Vietnamese vaccine usage-instruction leaflets (\emph{\vn{Tờ hướng dẫn sử dụng}}) collected from the Drug Administration of Vietnam (DAV) public service portal (\url{https://dichvucong.dav.gov.vn/congbothuoc/index}) on September 18, 2026. The initial registry query retrieved all 123 registered vaccine product listings. A systematic filtering protocol excluded 48 listings that lacked usable clinical text: 44 listings had no attached regulatory files, one failed due to a malformed download URL, two yielded empty directories, and one contained only packaging-label artwork without clinical usage instructions. The remaining 75 PDF documents correspond one-to-one with 75 canonical document identifiers (\artifact{doc_id}), each mapped to a unique vaccine product. In several instances, these official PDFs bundle marketing-authorization decisions or regulatory guidance alongside the clinical package insert.

Following PDF-to-Markdown conversion, HTML table reconstruction, and manual verification, the section parser and structure-aware chunker segment the 75 documents into 1,744 unique chunks. Each chunk is assigned a deterministic identifier formatted as \texttt{\{doc\_id\}\_c\_\{idx:04d\}}, alongside hierarchical section headers (\artifact{parent_header}, \artifact{header}), a content-type descriptor, a boolean table indicator (\artifact{metadata.has_table}), and a normalized medical category derived from 21 rule-based clinical heading patterns. By modality, 1,254 chunks (71.9\%) contain pure prose (\artifact{text}), 487 chunks (27.9\%) consist predominantly of tabular markup (\artifact{table}), and 3 chunks (0.2\%) combine prose with embedded tables (\artifact{mixed}), yielding 490 table-bearing chunks in total. \subsection{Stratified 300-Query Benchmark Construction}
\label{subsec:benchmark_construction}
To evaluate retrieval and grounded generation across diverse information-seeking scenarios, we constructed a 300-question Vietnamese benchmark. The benchmark was synthesized from the chunked corpus using DeepEval's context-grounded \artifact{Synthesizer} (one golden per context group, with expected outputs)~\cite{deepevalcontexts}, driven by \artifact{gpt-oss-120b} (temperature $0.2$, deterministic random seed $42$, generated on November 16, 2026).

Rather than sampling chunks uniformly—which would over-represent dense trial tables—the generator enforces a three-dimensional stratified design across three orthogonal axes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Answerability (70\% Answerable, 30\% Partially Answerable):} \emph{Answerable} queries ($n=210$) ask for clinical facts fully supported by the supplied context group. \emph{Partially Answerable} queries ($n=90$) are deliberately formulated as two-part inquiries: one clause is directly answerable from the reference context, whereas the second clause introduces an out-of-context clinical constraint or secondary question, testing whether the system can answer the supported component while explicitly abstaining from the unsupported portion.
\item \textbf{Reasoning Complexity (50\% Single-Hop, 30\% Multi-Hop, 20\% Table Reasoning):} \emph{Single-Hop} items ($n=150$) are synthesized from a single non-table prose chunk using \emph{Concretizing} ($0.5$) and \emph{Constrained} ($0.5$) query evolutions. \emph{Table Reasoning} items ($n=60$) target a single table-bearing chunk (\artifact{has_table=True}) under \emph{Reasoning} ($0.5$) and \emph{Comparative} ($0.5$) evolutions, requiring precise lookup or comparison of dosage intervals, age thresholds, seroprotection rates, or geometric mean titers (GMT). \emph{Multi-Hop} items ($n=90$) require synthesizing evidence across two distinct chunks and are balanced into an exact 50/50 split between two sub-types:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Intra-document multi-hop (\artifact{same_doc}, $n=45$):} Pairs two clinically complementary sections from the same vaccine leaflet across 11 predefined category pairs (e.g., \emph{Indications $\times$ Contraindications}, \emph{Dosage $\times$ Warnings}, \emph{Composition $\times$ Contraindications}), excluding uninformative overdose stubs under 200 characters, and evolves queries via \emph{Multicontext} ($0.7$) and \emph{Reasoning} ($0.3$) transformations.
\item \emph{Inter-vaccine comparative multi-hop (\artifact{cross_vaccine}, $n=45$):} Pairs chunks belonging to the same clinical category across two different vaccine products (spanning eight comparable domains such as storage, pregnancy, adverse reactions, or schedules) under \emph{Comparative} ($0.6$) and \emph{Multicontext} ($0.4$) evolutions, requiring explicit naming of both vaccines.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Query Style (60\% Patient Style, 40\% Clinical Style):} \emph{Patient Style} queries ($n=180$) emulate layperson inquiries on health forums or messaging platforms, featuring everyday Vietnamese phrasing, conversational particles, and brief personal context. \emph{Clinical Style} queries ($n=120$) emulate healthcare professionals verifying immunization protocols for complex patient cases with multiple clinical constraints (e.g., comorbidities, delayed booster intervals, concomitant medications).
\end{enumerate}

The joint allocation across the $2 \times 3 \times 2 = 12$ cells is computed via the Hamilton largest-remainder method followed by iterative marginal reconciliation, ensuring that the cell counts sum to 300 while preserving the exact target marginals along every axis (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}). Because DeepEval's asynchronous synthesis returns generated items out of input batch order, naive positional indexing in the initial export caused misalignment between each generated context and its recorded source chunk identifiers in 279 of the 300 records. To guarantee provenance integrity, we implemented an offline deterministic context-to-chunk reconciliation pass. This procedure matches each embedded context string against the canonical \texttt{[doc\_title | category | header]} formatting of the chunk store and enforces structural invariants (verifying that \artifact{same_doc} items reference exactly one \artifact{doc_id}, \artifact{cross_vaccine} items reference two distinct \artifact{doc_ids}, and \artifact{TABLE_REASONING} items include at least one chunk with \artifact{has_table=True}). The resulting benchmark achieves 100\% provenance alignment with the corpus and serves as the ground-truth benchmark for all retrieval and generation experiments.

For stress-testing complete abstention on wholly unanswerable queries, we construct a dedicated auxiliary set of unanswerable questions. The seed pool consists of 1,200 Vietnamese consumer health questions released in Stage~3 of the R2AI 2026 competition, most of which are not about vaccines. Each seed question is rewritten by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. The model receives the list of vaccines available in our corpus and must produce a natural Vietnamese question that names exactly one of these vaccines while preserving the clinical intent and any symptoms or conditions of the original query. Because the original concerns are largely unrelated to vaccination, most rewritten questions cannot be answered from the corresponding leaflet. From the resulting pool we retain 100 questions labeled \emph{Unanswerable}. These carry no gold source chunks, since the expected behavior is abstention rather than retrieval.

\subsection{Expert Review and Quality Assurance Protocol}
\label{subsec:expert_review_protocol}
To audit label validity, reference-answer accuracy, and evidence completeness, we developed a dedicated web-based clinical review platform backed by a stratified three-expert allocation protocol. As detailed in the right-hand columns of Table~\ref{tab:dataset}, the 300 benchmark questions are partitioned deterministically (seed $42$) into four functional batches across three medical reviewers:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Calibration Batch (15 questions, 45 review turns):} A stratified 5\% sample evaluated independently by all three experts prior to consensus discussion, aligning interpretation of the annotation rubric and partial-answerability boundaries.
\item \textbf{Inter-Annotator Overlap Batch (30 questions, 90 review turns):} A stratified 10\% sample scored independently and blindly by all three reviewers to quantify inter-annotator agreement (IAA). The platform computes Fleiss' Kappa ($\kappa$)~\cite{fleiss1971measuring} with Landis and Koch agreement tiers~\cite{landis1977measurement} across two dimensions—three-class answerability classification and binary chunk-level evidence selection—supplemented by mean pairwise Jaccard similarity across the selected evidence sets.
\item \textbf{Independent Primary Batch (255 questions, 85 per reviewer):} The remaining 85\% of the benchmark is divided equally into three balanced, non-overlapping packages of 85 questions per expert, preserving identical marginal proportions across all 12 strata.
\item \textbf{Risk-Prioritized Cross-Audit Batch (45 questions, 15 per reviewer):} To safeguard high-complexity items within the independent packages, 15 questions from each expert's primary allocation (45 questions in total) are routed in a round-robin schedule (each expert's items are audited by the next expert in cyclic order) to a second expert for cross-auditing. The risk-based selection rule concentrates 100\% of these cross-audit slots on the most error-prone strata—specifically \emph{Table Reasoning} (30 audited items) and \emph{Partially Answerable} non-table items (15 audited items)—yielding exactly 145 total evaluation turns per reviewer (435 turns overall).
\end{enumerate}

Within the review interface, experts inspect the generated question, expected answer, and highlighted source chunks alongside a searchable full-document view. Reviewers can approve an item as-is (\artifact{APPROVED}), modify the question, reference answer, answerability label, or supporting chunk list with structured rationales (\artifact{EDITED}), or flag malformed synthetic items (\artifact{REJECTED}), thereby establishing an auditable provenance chain from raw regulatory PDFs to benchmark evaluation.

\paragraph{Comparative analysis of retrieval progression.}
Tables~\ref{tab:ir-overall} and~\ref{tab:ir-complexity} highlight three principal findings across the five retrieval architectures:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Hybrid sparse-dense fusion and cross-encoder reranking:} Transitioning from single-vector dense retrieval (Advanced) to the Hybrid baseline yields a $+15.33$ percentage-point gain in overall Hit@5 ($0.5900 \rightarrow 0.7433$) and a $+14.68$ point gain in MRR@5 ($0.4064 \rightarrow 0.5532$). Gains are observed across all complexity strata: $+18.66$ points in Hit@5 on single-hop queries, $+18.33$ points on table-reasoning queries, and $+7.77$ points on multi-hop queries.
\item \textbf{Table consolidation and dual-representation indexing:} On the 60-query \emph{Table Reasoning} stratum, Table-aware retrieval elevates Hit@1 from $0.1167$ to $0.4667$ ($+35.00$ points), Hit@3 from $0.4667$ to $0.7667$ ($+30.00$ points), Hit@5 from $0.5833$ to $0.7667$ ($+18.34$ points), and MRR@5 from $0.3044$ to $0.5972$ ($+29.28$ points). Notably, scoring the table-consolidated index under strict primary-ID matching without merged sub-chunk alias resolution artificially depresses overall Hit@5 to $0.7233$ ($-2.00$ points below Hybrid), because relevant sub-chunks absorbed into a merged table are counted as misses when returned under the table's primary chunk identifier. Restoring rank-preserving alias resolution confirms that Table-aware improves overall Hit@1 by $+7.00$ points ($0.4233 \rightarrow 0.4933$) and overall Hit@5 by $+4.00$ points ($0.7433 \rightarrow 0.7833$) over Hybrid, while also lifting multi-hop Hit@5 from $0.7333$ to $0.7556$ ($+2.23$ points). Gains are particularly pronounced on clinical-style queries ($n=120$), where Hit@1 rises by $+10.00$ points ($0.4083 \rightarrow 0.5083$) and Hit@5 by $+6.66$ points ($0.7417 \rightarrow 0.8083$).
\item \textbf{Rank stability under Graph RAG passage fusion:} Incorporating provenance-resolved passages from the knowledge graph into the candidate pool prior to cross-encoder reranking produces identical binary coverage metrics to Table-aware across every cutoff and complexity stratum ($\text{Hit@1} = 0.4933$, $\text{Hit@3} = 0.7300$, $\text{Hit@5} = 0.7833$, $\text{Recall@5} = 0.7167$, and $\text{Precision@5} = 0.1753$). A query-by-query comparison shows that cross-branch RRF alters the final top-$5$ documentary ranking order in only $9$ of the $300$ benchmark queries ($3.0\%$: $4$ single-hop, $3$ multi-hop, and $2$ table-reasoning queries). Because both the passage index and the knowledge-graph vector collections share the same BGE-M3 embedding space and all fused candidates are re-scored by the same cross-encoder reranker, the top-$30$ hybrid candidate pool already captures the high-affinity documentary chunks, leaving only minor rank-order swaps (shifting overall MRR@5 from $0.6114$ to $0.6108$ and NDCG@5 from $0.6122$ to $0.6115$).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Impact of knowledge-graph context on answer synthesis and abstention.}
Because top-$5$ passage retrieval is already saturated after table-aware consolidation and cross-encoder reranking, the primary contribution of Graph RAG operates at the generation stage through the structured entity-relationship context block appended to the prompt. Across comparative end-to-end generation experiments evaluated with Qwen3.8-27B on the 300-query main benchmark and the 100-query auxiliary unanswerable benchmark:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Multi-source synthesis and explicit graph attribution:} On the 300-query benchmark, the knowledge-graph branch supplies a non-empty structured entity-relationship block for $100\%$ of queries ($300/300$). The generator produces $827$ valid inline citations ($2.76$ valid citations per response) compared to $847$ valid citations in the Hybrid baseline, and explicitly cites the knowledge-graph context alongside documentary chunk identifiers in $71$ of the $300$ responses ($23.7\%$) to consolidate cross-section entity attributes and relational constraints.
\item \textbf{Abstention robustness and latency trade-off:} On the 100-query auxiliary unanswerable set, Graph RAG achieves an explicit clinical refusal rate of $58.0\%$ ($58/100$), matching the Hybrid baseline ($59.0\%$, $59/100$). This equivalence demonstrates that providing a curated relational subgraph does not induce spurious compliance on out-of-scope clinical symptoms. Conversely, executing LLM-based query keyword extraction and dual Neo4j/Qdrant traversal increases mean end-to-end response latency on the 300-query benchmark from $42.33\,\text{s}$ per query under Hybrid to $65.40\,\text{s}$ per query under Graph RAG.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Graph Schema and Constraints}
\label{app:graph_schema}
The vaccine knowledge graph enforces a twelve-class biomedical ontology tailored to Vietnamese regulatory package inserts. During extraction, the language model classifies each identified clinical concept into one of twelve canonical types—\emph{Vaccine}, \emph{Ingredient}, \emph{Pathogen}, \emph{Indication}, \emph{Contraindication}, \emph{Adverse\_Reaction}, \emph{Clinical\_Outcome}, \emph{Target\_Population}, \emph{Dosage\_And\_Route}, \emph{Precaution}, \emph{Drug\_Interaction}, or \emph{Storage\_Condition} (defaulting to \emph{Other} when a concept falls outside these categories)—and emits structured JSON objects containing typed entity descriptions and directed relational triplets (Listing~\ref{lst:vax-entity-ontology}).

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Biomedical entity ontology and JSON output schema used for vaccine knowledge-graph extraction.},label={lst:vax-entity-ontology}]
Entity Ontology Categories (12 Clinical Classes):
  - Vaccine: Commercial product names and proprietary vaccine formulations.
  - Ingredient: Active antigens, toxoids, viral/bacterial strains, adjuvants (e.g., AS01B, aluminum hydroxide), excipients, and manufacturing residues.
  - Pathogen: Causative viruses, bacteria, or specific serotypes/serogroups.
  - Indication: Prophylactic diseases, syndromes, or clinical complications prevented.
  - Contraindication: Absolute medical exclusions, hypersensitivity history, or acute febrile states.
  - Adverse_Reaction: Local and systemic post-vaccination adverse events.
  - Clinical_Outcome: Immunogenicity endpoints (GMT, GMC), seroprotection rates, and protective efficacy.
  - Target_Population: Recommended age cohorts, pregnant/lactating women, preterm infants, or immunocompromised groups.
  - Dosage_And_Route: Dose volume (e.g., 0.5 ml), administration route (IM/SC), anatomical site, and primary/booster schedules.
  - Precaution: Pre-vaccination screening and post-vaccination clinical observation requirements.
  - Drug_Interaction: Concomitant vaccines, immunosuppressive therapies, or immunoglobulin interactions.
  - Storage_Condition: Cold-chain temperature ranges (2-8 C), light protection, freezing prohibition, and post-reconstitution shelf life.
